# SCHOOL Design System Direction

## Theme

Design language: Academic Precision / Calm Ambition.

The interface should feel:

- calm
- premium
- precise
- mobile-first
- serious enough for academic work

It should not feel playful, noisy, inflated, or startup-generic.

## Experience Goals

1. Reduce cognitive drag.
2. Make the next decision obvious.
3. Let important actions stand out without visual shouting.
4. Preserve a sense of institutional trust and seriousness.

## Visual Principles

### Calm Over Flash

Use restrained contrast, strong spacing, and clear typographic hierarchy.

### Precision Over Decoration

Every visual element should help orientation, priority, or comprehension.

### Decision-First Layout

Top-of-screen real estate should go to:

- next action
- risk
- due items
- assessment paths
- support escalation

### Premium Through Restraint

Premium here means disciplined typography, surface quality, spacing, and copy tone, not ornamental excess.

## Layout Rules

- Prefer single clear primary action per section.
- Make "today" and "next" visible above exploratory content.
- On mobile, stack by urgency: action, due, review, planner, support.
- Keep high-value cards short and scannable.
- Avoid wide undifferentiated grids on primary screens.

## Component Guidance

### Command Cards

Used for next-best actions, due reviews, past-paper prompts, planner prompts, and help escalation.

Required anatomy:

- small eyebrow
- short directive title
- one sentence of consequence or payoff
- one primary action
- optional secondary metadata

### Progress Language

Prefer:

- "Needs review"
- "Developing"
- "Confident"
- "Ready for past paper"
- "At risk this week"

Avoid sterile or punitive language.

### Empty States

Empty states should explain what unlocks the area next.

Bad: "No data."
Good: "Complete a quiz or past paper and SCHOOL will surface review priorities here."

## Tokens Direction

Current implementation can keep the existing dark premium shell, but future token work should push toward:

- softer neutral surfaces
- less saturated gold usage
- stronger information hierarchy
- clearer semantic states for risk, review, confidence, and completion

Gold should signal significance, not paint every primary surface.

## Typography Direction

- Display type should be used sparingly for page-level emphasis.
- Body copy should remain highly readable on mobile.
- Monospace should signal system state, counts, periods, and metadata.

## Motion

- Use subtle reveal and emphasis.
- Avoid constant motion.
- Motion should reinforce hierarchy, not seek attention.

## Design Review Checklist

- Can a student tell what matters in under 5 seconds?
- Is the best next action obvious?
- Is the screen helping execution rather than browsing?
- Is assessment visible enough?
- Does the copy sound academically credible?
- Would this still feel premium on a small phone screen?
