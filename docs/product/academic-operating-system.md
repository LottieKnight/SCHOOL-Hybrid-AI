# SCHOOL Product Direction

## Position

SCHOOL is not a collection of disconnected education features.

SCHOOL should become a personal academic operating system: the place where a student decides what matters now, studies against real assessments, sees proof of progress, coordinates time, gets support, and compounds advantage over time.

The product should feel academically serious, operationally useful, and personally adaptive.

## Product Thesis

The winning behavior is not "more content consumed".

The winning behavior is:

1. The student knows what to do next.
2. The student practices against real assessment pressure.
3. The system remembers weak and strong areas.
4. The planner and support layer react to that memory.
5. The student can prove progress to self, family, tutor, and future buyers of help.

This means SCHOOL should optimize for decision quality, study consistency, and measurable academic outcomes.

## Strategic Pillars

### 1. Student Command Center

The dashboard becomes the command surface for today, this week, and the current exam horizon.

It should answer:

- What is the next best academic action?
- What is at risk?
- What needs review?
- Which assessment should I do next?
- Where should I ask for help or buy support?

### 2. Assessment-First Learning

Assessment should anchor the product, not sit as a side feature.

Past papers, timed practice, memo review, weakness detection, and remediation loops should shape the main study flow.

### 3. Learner Memory and Mastery

SCHOOL should remember what the learner recently studied, where confidence is rising, and what needs review.

Expose this in humane language:

- "Needs review"
- "Developing"
- "Confident"

Do not over-index on raw mastery numbers in the primary UI.

### 4. AI Study Coach

The assistant should become an AI study coach tied to academic context:

- explain
- diagnose
- quiz
- plan
- redirect to resources
- escalate to tutor help when appropriate

### 5. Planner Excellence

The planner is not a calendar toy. It is an execution layer for deadlines, exams, revision blocks, and study commitments.

Planner quality matters because it connects intention to outcome.

### 6. Social Learning

Community should remain academically anchored:

- module rooms
- question-and-answer support
- accepted answers
- peer explanation
- cohort motivation

Avoid drifting toward generic social engagement mechanics.

### 7. Proof of Progress

Students need visible evidence that effort is working.

Proof surfaces should include:

- recent assessment results
- mastery movement
- review consistency
- streaks and completion
- tutor-ready or parent-shareable summaries

### 8. Outcome-Driven Tutor Marketplace

Tutors should be framed as outcome support, not directory inventory.

The marketplace should eventually connect:

- diagnosed weakness
- needed outcome
- tutor suitability
- proof of improvement after sessions

### 9. Resource Intelligence

Resources should be ranked by relevance to current academic needs, not just listed.

Resource intelligence should connect the student's context, planner, memory, and assessments.

## Product Principles

- Decision-first over browse-first.
- Assessment-led over content-led.
- Memory-aware over stateless.
- Outcome-linked over activity-linked.
- Calm, precise, and serious over loud or gamified.
- Compounding advantage over feature sprawl.

## Near-Term Implications For Repo Work

- Strengthen the dashboard into a real command center.
- Keep past papers highly visible and operational.
- Express memory/mastery in practical language.
- Tighten planner and review loops before inventing more surfaces.
- Document IA and design rules so future work compounds.
